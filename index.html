Clinical decision rule assessing active elbow extension after acute trauma to screen for fracture and determine whether radiography can be safely deferred.

Physiotutors: Elbow Extension Test | Olecranon Fracture · Open on YouTube

How to perform

  1. Seat or stand the patient with the injured arm exposed and supinated.
  2. Ask the patient to actively extend both elbows fully and compare symmetry with the uninjured side.
  3. Also check active flexion, pronation, and supination and palpate the olecranon directly.
  4. Decision rule in words: if the patient achieves full active extension and an olecranon fracture is not suspected, radiography can be deferred with safety-net advice; if active extension is incomplete, refer for radiograph.

Positive / result

Inability to fully extend the elbow actively after acute injury (or limited active flexion, extension, pronation, or supination).

Interpretation

Appelboam 2008 (adults + children, n = 1740): Sn 96.8% (95.0–98.2), Sp 48.5% (45.6–51.4); worst-case Sn 95.3%; Sp similar in adults (47.7%) and children (49.5%). In adults, full extension gives NPV 98.4% (96.3–99.5), LR− 0.03 (0.01–0.08); radiography can be deferred if an olecranon fracture is not suspected, and patients not X-rayed "should return if symptoms have not resolved within 7-10 days". Lennon 2007: for detecting a normal radiograph, full extension Sp 0.916 (0.863–0.969), Sn 0.478; equal ROM Sp 0.976 (0.940–0.991), Sn 0.211; under 16 years equal ROM Sp 1 (0.941–1.000). Darracq 2008 (n = 113): limited active ROM (flexion, extension, pronation, supination) Sn 100% (0.93–1.00) for fracture or effusion; preserved active ROM Sp 97% (0.89–1.00) for absence of fracture.

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: High

SN: 96.8% (95.0–98.2) Appelboam; 100% (0.93–1.00) Darracq limited AROM · SP: 48.5% (45.6–51.4) Appelboam; normal radiograph Sp 0.916 full ext / 0.976 equal ROM (Lennon); 97% (0.89–1.00) Darracq · NPV: 98.4% (96.3–99.5) in adults · LR_NEG: 0.03 (0.01–0.08) in adults

Dossier p1 Triage & red flags · p2 Differential & prognosis

Clinical red flags requiring emergency medical or orthopaedic referral and proximal referral sources mimicking elbow pathology.

Key points

  1. Dislocation with neurovascular compromise: gross deformity after trauma with absent/diminished radial or ulnar pulse, pallor, or acute median/ulnar/radial nerve deficit → emergency reduction and vascular assessment.
  2. Forearm compartment syndrome: severe pain out of proportion to injury, tense swollen forearm compartments, pain on passive finger extension, paraesthesia, or paresis → emergency surgical decompression.
  3. Septic olecranon bursitis or septic arthritis: acute hot, red, swollen olecranon bursa or elbow joint with fever, systemic malaise, or overlying skin breach → urgent aspiration/medical referral.
  4. Fracture in a child: acute trauma with refusal to use the arm, swelling, or incomplete active ROM → paediatric radiographic assessment.
  5. Cardiac referral to the left medial arm: non-mechanical medial arm/elbow ache associated with exertional chest tightness, dyspnoea, or jaw pain → emergency cardiac workup.
  6. Cervical radiculopathy referral: lateral or medial elbow pain reproduced by neck movement or accompanied by proximal arm paraesthesia/myotomal weakness → cervical spine and neurodynamic screen.

Positive / result

Any red flag present mandates immediate or urgent medical/orthopaedic referral prior to mechanical rehabilitation.

Interpretation

Expert consensus triage gate ensuring limb-threatening, infectious, paediatric fracture, visceral cardiac, and cervical radicular presentations are identified at intake.

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: Expert opinion

Emergency / urgent referral screen (E)

Dossier page 1 · Triage & red flags

Clinical differentiation of medial flexor-pronator tendinopathy, radial tunnel / posterior interosseous nerve entrapment, capitellar osteochondritis dissecans, and elbow osteoarthritis / post-traumatic stiffness.

Key points

  1. Medial epicondylalgia (golfer's elbow): insidious medial epicondyle pain aggravated by resisted wrist flexion and forearm pronation; tender over the common flexor-pronator origin just distal/anterior to the medial epicondyle; differentiate from UCL insufficiency and cubital tunnel syndrome.
  2. Radial tunnel / posterior interosseous nerve (PIN) syndrome: deep aching pain over the mobile wad / supinator mass distal to the lateral epicondyle (radial tunnel) or pure motor weakness of finger/thumb extension without sensory loss (PIN).
  3. Osteochondritis dissecans (OCD) of the capitellum: lateral elbow pain, catching, locking, or effusion in adolescent overhead throwers or gymnasts exacerbated by valgus compression.
  4. Osteoarthritis / post-traumatic stiffness: global or capsular loss of flexion-extension and/or forearm rotation, end-range impingement pain, or crepitus after trauma or degenerative joint disease.

Positive / result

Pattern recognition guides targeted special testing and imaging referral when mechanical locking or motor denervation is present.

Interpretation

Expert clinical differentiation across lateral, medial, anterior, and posterior elbow compartments.

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: Expert opinion

Clinical differential signposts (E)

Dossier page 2 · Differential & prognosis

Population prevalence, occupational and smoking risk factors, and 52-week RCT trajectories comparing wait-and-see, physiotherapy, and corticosteroid injection.

Key points

  1. Prevalence (Shiri 2006, age 30–64, n = 4,783): definite lateral epicondylitis 1.3%, medial 0.4%; highest at 45–54 years; no sex difference.
  2. Risk factors for lateral epicondylitis (Shiri 2006): current smoking OR 3.4 (1.4–8.3); former smoking OR 3.0 (1.3–6.6); repetitive + forceful arm activity OR 5.6 (1.9–16.5).
  3. Natural course vs treatment (Smidt 2002 Lancet RCT, n = 185): success at 6 weeks: injection 92%, physiotherapy 47%, wait-and-see 32%; at 52 weeks: injection 69%, physiotherapy 91%, wait-and-see 83%.
  4. MWM + exercise vs injection vs wait-and-see (Bisset 2006 BMJ RCT, n = 198): at 52 weeks injection worse than physiotherapy (0.3, 0.1 to 0.5; NNT 4) and than wait-and-see on 2 of 3 measures (0.3, 0.04 to 0.4; NNT 4); after injection 47/65 (72%) deteriorated after 3 or 6 weeks.

Positive / result

High 52-week recovery with wait-and-see (83%) and physiotherapy (91%), whereas corticosteroid injection shows high recurrence (72%).

Interpretation

Counselling patients on the favourable 52-week natural course and high post-injection recurrence rate is central to shared decision-making.

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: High

Prevalence LET 1.3%, medial 0.4%; repetitive + forceful OR 5.6 (1.9–16.5); 52-wk success physio 91%, wait-and-see 83%, injection 69% (NNT 4)

Dossier p2 Differential & prognosis · p3 Objective exam

Core physical impairment measure for lateral epicondylalgia assessing pain-free grip force, maximum grip force, and limb symmetry ratio.

How to perform

  1. Position the patient with the elbow in standardized extension (or repeatable test posture) holding a calibrated hand-held dynamometer.
  2. For pain-free grip (PFG): instruct the patient to squeeze slowly and stop at the very first onset of elbow pain; record in kg.
  3. For maximum grip: instruct the patient to squeeze maximally; record in kg and rate pain on gripping (0–10).
  4. Compute the affected ÷ unaffected × 100 (%) ratio and compare longitudinal change against the Sveinall 2025 SDC95 and MIC values.

Positive / result

Reduced pain-free grip on the affected side relative to the unaffected side; improvement ≥ 6.5 kg (MIC) / ≥ 6.4 kg (SDC95) indicates meaningful change.

Interpretation

Smidt 2002 Arch Phys Med Rehabil: interobserver ICC pain-free grip .97, maximum grip .98, severity of complaints .90, pressure pain threshold .77 (unsatisfactory). Sveinall 2025: SDC95 6.4 kg pain-free grip, 8.4 kg maximum grip; MIC 6.5 kg pain-free, 1 kg maximum; pain on gripping SDC95 4, MIC 3.5 (0–10).

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: Moderate

ICC: PFG .97, max grip .98, severity .90, PPT .77 (Smidt 2002) · MDC: SDC95: 6.4 kg PFG, 8.4 kg max grip, 4 pain on gripping (0–10) (Sveinall 2025) · MCID: MIC: 6.5 kg PFG, 1 kg max grip, 3.5 pain on gripping (0–10) (Sveinall 2025)

Universal goniometer intra-rater and inter-rater real-change thresholds for elbow flexion, extension, pronation, and supination.

Key points

  1. Align the universal goniometer over the lateral epicondyle (humeral shaft stationary arm, radial shaft moving arm) for flexion and extension.

Change exceeding the intra-rater or inter-rater threshold represents real change in joint motion.

JOSPT LET CPG (Lucado 2022): "Clinicians should use isometric, concentric, and/or eccentric therapeutic resisted exercises of the wrist extensors in the treatment of individuals with subacute or chronic LET"; "should use therapeutic resisted wrist extension strengthening exercises in combination with other therapeutic interventions, including manual therapy"; "may use a phased approach to reintroduce stress, increase strength, improve endurance, and restore optimal motor control" in high-demand occupations, hobbies, performing arts or sport; "may include shoulder and scapular stabilizer muscle training exercises, when impairments are identified".
  2. Natural course comparison: at 52 weeks physiotherapy 91% vs wait-and-see 83% success (Smidt 2002); MWM + exercise vs no physio 91% vs 88% at 1 year (Coombes 2013).
  3. Strengthening vs no treatment MA (Kim 2021): pain MD −0.07 (−0.56 to 0.41), function SMD −0.08 (−0.46 to 0.30).
  4. Also address medial epicondylalgia (progressive flexor-pronator loading), kinetic-chain shoulder/scapular control, and graded return to work/sport (E).

Positive / result

Multimodal resisted wrist extensor loading (isometric, concentric, eccentric) combined with manual therapy and kinetic-chain training.

Interpretation

Match exercise contraction mode and load to symptom irritability and occupational/sporting demand.

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: High

52-wk success: physio 91% vs wait-and-see 83% (Smidt 2002); MWM + exercise 91% vs 88% at 1 y (Coombes 2013); Kim 2021 pain MD −0.07, function SMD −0.08
