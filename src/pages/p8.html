Wrist extensor loading (Lucado 2022 JOSPT LET CPG) (Guideline): “Clinicians should use isometric, concentric, and/or eccentric therapeutic resisted exercises of the wrist extensors in the treatment of individuals with subacute or chronic LET”
Multimodal combination (Lucado 2022 JOSPT LET CPG) (Guideline): “should use therapeutic resisted wrist extension strengthening exercises in combination with other therapeutic interventions, including manual therapy”
Phased return & kinetic chain (Lucado 2022 JOSPT LET CPG) (Guideline): “may use a phased approach to reintroduce stress, increase strength, improve endurance, and restore optimal motor control” in high-demand occupations, hobbies, performing arts or sport; “may include shoulder and scapular stabilizer muscle training exercises, when impairments are identified”
Strengthening MA, Natural Course, UCL & StiffnessEvidence
Strengthening vs no treatment (MA) (Kim 2021 JSES Int) M: no difference in pain (MD −0.07) or function (SMD −0.08) — see page 6
Natural course comparison (Smidt 2002 Lancet · Coombes 2013 JAMA) H: at 52 weeks physiotherapy 91% vs wait-and-see 83% success (Smidt 2002, page 2); MWM + exercise vs no physio 91% vs 88% at 1 year (Coombes 2013)
UCL non-operative rehab (Erickson 2015 Sports Health) L: “Nonoperative treatment should be attempted, but limited studies have not shown promising results” · UCL post-op (Kemler 2022) M: interval throwing programme in 81.8% of protocols; RTS 6.5 to 16 months
Post-traumatic stiffness (Lindenhovius 2012) H: exercise + splinting improves over six to twelve months · Medial epicondylalgia, loading specifics & RTW: graded flexor-pronator loading, isometric/eccentric/heavy slow resistance progression & ergonomic task modification E

Pain-monitoring model: Sprague 2021 E: pain should not exceed 5/10 on the NPRS during or immediately after activity and should return to pre-activity levels by the following morning • Silbernagel 2007 M: continued tendon loading with a pain-monitoring model showed no negative effects
