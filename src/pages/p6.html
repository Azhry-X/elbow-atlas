A · Corticosteroid, PRP & Autologous Blood Injections in Lateral EpicondylalgiaINJECTIONS
Corticosteroid Injection (Bisset 2006 · Coombes 2010 & 2013)Coombes 2013
Corticosteroid injection (Bisset) (Bisset 2006 BMJ) H: better at 6 weeks, but “paradoxically reversed after six weeks, with high recurrence rates”; worse than physiotherapy at 52 weeks (NNT 4) — see page 2
Corticosteroid injection (SR, 41 RCTs) (Coombes 2010 Lancet) H: lateral epicondylalgia: large short-term pain effect vs no intervention (SMD 1·44, 1·17–1·71) but no intervention favoured at intermediate term (−0·40, −0·67 to −0·14) and long term (−0·31, −0·61 to −0·01); serious adverse event (tendon rupture) in 1 of 991 (0·1%)
Corticosteroid vs placebo injection ± physio (RCT, n = 165) (Coombes 2013 JAMA) H: 1 year: recovery 83% vs 96% (RR 0.86, 99% CI 0.75–0.99); recurrence 54% vs 12% (RR 0.23, 0.10–0.51); 26 weeks: 55% vs 85% (RR 0.79, 0.62–0.99)
PRP, Autologous Blood & Non-Operative MAKarjalainen 2021
PRP / autologous blood (Cochrane, 8 studies) (Karjalainen 2021 Cochrane) H: vs placebo at 3 months: pain 0.16 points better (0–10), function 2 points (0–100) — not clinically important
Non-operative treatments (MA) (Kim 2021 JSES Int) M: PRP vs control: function SMD 0.14 (−0.45 to 0.73); autologous blood vs control: function −0.07 (−0.64 to 0.50); strengthening vs no treatment: pain MD −0.07 (−0.56 to 0.41), function SMD −0.08 (−0.46 to 0.30); “does not support the use of nonoperative treatment options including physiotherapy (strengthening), CSI, PRP, or AB”
B · ESWT, Laser, Orthoses, Taping & JOSPT 2022 LET CPG Modalities RecommendationsEPAS & ORTHOSES
ESWT, Low-Level Laser & Orthoses ReviewsBjordal 2008
ESWT (Cochrane) (Buchbinder 2005 Cochrane) M: one trial (93 participants): steroid injection may be more effective than ESWT
Low-level laser (MA) (Bjordal 2008 BMC Musculoskelet Disord) M: vs control: pain WMD 10.2 mm (3.0 to 17.5) on 100 mm VAS; 904 nm directly on the tendon (5 trials): 17.2 mm (8.5 to 25.9) more than placebo; pain-free grip SMD 0.66 (0.42 to 0.90)
Orthoses / counterforce brace (Cochrane) (Struijs 2002 Cochrane) L: “No definitive conclusions can be drawn concerning effectiveness of orthotic devices”
JOSPT LET CPG — Orthoses & Taping (Lucado 2022)JOSPT 2022
Counterforce / wrist orthosis (Lucado 2022 JOSPT LET CPG) (Guideline): “Clinicians may use a forearm counterforce or wrist support orthosis to be worn during activity for immediate improvement of pain and strength in those with LET whose symptoms are aggravated with activity”; for intermediate or long-term symptoms: “a recommendation cannot be made”
Rigid & kinesiology taping (Lucado 2022 JOSPT LET CPG) (Guideline): “Clinicians should use rigid taping techniques for immediate/short-term pain relief and improvement in pain-free muscle function in those with irritable LET”; “Clinicians may use kinesiology tape application as part of a multimodal treatment program for immediate and short-term management of pain and muscle function”
JOSPT LET CPG — Electrophysical Agents (Lucado 2022)JOSPT 2022
Ultrasound (Lucado 2022 JOSPT LET CPG) (Guideline): “a recommendation cannot be made for the use of ultrasound as a stand-alone treatment”
Phonophoresis (Lucado 2022 JOSPT LET CPG) (Guideline): “Clinicians should not use phonophoresis with 10% hydrocortisone gel, topical prednisolone (2 mg/d), or 1% diclofenac sodium gel”
Iontophoresis (Lucado 2022 JOSPT LET CPG) (Guideline): “may use iontophoresis with an anti-inflammatory drug, early in the rehabilitation phase (no later than 2–4 weeks from onset or aggravation of symptoms), in individuals presenting with highly irritable symptoms”
Laser therapy (Lucado 2022 JOSPT LET CPG) (Guideline): “may use laser therapy for improvements in pain and grip strength, seen in follow-up periods >4 weeks to 6 months”
Cryotherapy + burst TENS (Lucado 2022 JOSPT LET CPG) (Guideline): “may use cryotherapy combined with burst transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) to reduce pain in the short term in individuals with symptoms of LET for greater than 30 days”
