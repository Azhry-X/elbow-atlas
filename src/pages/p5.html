Distal Biceps Repair (Amarasooriya 2020 · Amin 2016 · Bergman 2021)Distal Biceps
Complications (SR) (Amarasooriya 2020 Am J Sports Med) M: major complications 4.6%; minor 20.4% (n = 630); most common lateral (antebrachial) cutaneous nerve injury 9.2% (n = 283); LABCN injury higher with limited anterior vs extensile single incision (9.7% vs 5.2%, P = .03); superficial radial nerve injury higher with extensile vs limited incision (6% vs 2.1%, P = .002); fixation technique had no effect on rerupture or PIN injury
Single vs double incision (MA) (Amin 2016 Orthop J Sports Med) M: LABCN neurapraxia most common after single incision: 77 of 785 (9.8%)
Early motion vs immobilisation (RCT, cortical button) (Bergman 2021 J Bone Joint Surg Am) H: no difference in return to work (p ≥ 0.83); “No clinically important group differences were seen”
Surgery vs Sham for LET (Kroslak 2018)LET Surgery
Surgery vs sham for LET (RCT, n = 26) (Kroslak 2018 Am J Sports Med) H: no significant difference in any parameter at any stage; > 6500 patients per group would be needed to show a difference
UCL Reconstruction & Internal Brace RepairErickson 2015
UCL reconstruction (SR, 20 studies, 2019 athletes) (Erickson 2015 Orthop J Sports Med) M: pooled return to sport 86.2%; 90% excellent/good (Conway-Jobe); RTS collegiate 95.5% vs high school 89.4% vs professional 86.4%; docking 97.0%, ASMI 93.3%, Jobe 66.7%
UCL reconstruction — large series (Cain 2010 Am J Sports Med, 743 athletes ≥ 2 y) L: 83% returned to the same or higher level in less than 1 year
UCL repair with internal brace (Dugas 2019 Am J Sports Med, 111 throwers) L: 92% (102/111) returned to the same or higher level at a mean 6.7 months; mean KJOC 88.2
UCL reconstruction — rehab & RTS criteria (SR, 33 studies, 3480 athletes) (Kemler 2022 Am J Sports Med) M: RTS timelines 6.5 to 16 months; protocols most often used bracing with progressive ROM (93.9%), strengthening (84.8%) and an interval throwing programme (81.8%); no study fully defined RTS criteria
B · Post-Traumatic Stiffness / Arthrolysis, Heterotopic Ossification & Radial Head FracturesTRAUMA & STIFFNESS
Post-Traumatic Stiffness: Arthrolysis & SplintingLanzerath 2023
Arthrolysis (SR, 30 studies, 798 pts) (Kodde 2013 J Shoulder Elbow Surg) M: ROM gain 51°, 40°, 88°, 56° and complications 23, 5, 73, 58% for open, arthroscopic, open + external fixator, open + distraction arthroplasty
Arthrolysis (SR, 27 studies, 1666 pts) (Lanzerath 2023 Arch Orthop Trauma Surg) M: success (MEPS excellent/good): open 88.8% (revision 6.3%, other complications 18.1%); arthroscopic 91.8% (revision 1.6%, complications 9.1%)
Splinting for post-traumatic stiffness (RCT, n = 66) (Lindenhovius 2012 J Bone Joint Surg Am) H: no difference between static progressive and dynamic splinting; stiffness “can improve with exercises and dynamic or static splinting over a period of six to twelve months, and patience is warranted”
Heterotopic Ossification (Shukla 2015 · Khan 2019)HO Risk
Heterotopic ossification (Shukla 2015 J Shoulder Elbow Surg) L: after surgical fracture-dislocation 12 of 28 elbows (43%); 7 of 9 (77%) after multiple closed reductions
HO after total elbow arthroplasty (SR) (Khan 2019 J Shoulder Elbow Surg) M: radiographic 10%, symptomatic 3%
Radial Head & Mason I–II Fracture TrialsHarding 2011
Radial head fracture — early mobilisation (RCT, n = 60) (Liow 2002 Injury) M: immediate mobilisation: less pain and better function at 1 week, no harm
Radial head fracture — protocols (RCT, n = 180) (Paschos 2013 J Orthop Trauma) M: early mobilisation safe and effective; a delay of 48 hours before early mobilisation “could be advantageous”
Elbow fractures — early mobilisation (Cochrane) (Harding 2011 Cochrane) L: no difference between early and delayed mobilisation in pain or ROM limitation (Mason I–II); all participants returned to full ADL, none changed occupation
Mason II — operative vs non-operative (RCT) (Mulders 2021 J Shoulder Elbow Surg) M: similar function at 1 year
